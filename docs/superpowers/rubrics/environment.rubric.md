## Baseline

### env-greybox-metric-gameplay-first

The build shows evidence that geometry was authored for player FLOW and traversal, not improvised as decoration. PASS requires demonstrable function-first spatial design: navigation is readable and human-scaled, the layout guides the player along intended routes (lanes, paths, or framed approaches to destinations), and openings/clearances are sized so a character can actually move through them. The build need not recite exact stud metrics, but it MUST show the layout was shaped by traversal intent rather than placed arbitrarily. A flat field of hand-placed default parts, a single undesigned A-to-B corridor, or geometry that ignores how a player moves through it FAILS.

- **Why above generic:** The mean artifact is a flat baseplate of hand-placed default Parts with no layout intent at all — it cannot demonstrate flow, readable navigation, or traversal-sized openings. The great artifact explicitly delivers 'human-scale proportions and readable navigation' and 'guiding player flow', so it clears a flow-evidence bar. The original failed both because it demanded exact stud gates and dual-spawn structure the great artifact's outcome-level description never recites; re-anchoring on demonstrated flow/navigation (which only deliberate design produces) discriminates without requiring jargon recitation.
- **Pass:** Geometry is authored for flow: readable human-scaled navigation, routes that guide the player to intended destinations, and openings/clearances sized for character traversal — the layout was clearly shaped by how players move.
- **Partial:** Some flow/navigation intent is evident (a guided route or human-scaled openings) but it is incomplete — parts of the layout are arbitrary, or traversal sizing/route-guidance is inconsistent.
- **Fail:** A flat field of arbitrarily placed parts, a single undesigned A-to-B corridor, or geometry that ignores player traversal — no demonstrable flow design.
- **Evidence required:** A statement/capture showing geometry shaped for player flow: readable, human-scaled navigation, routes that guide the player to destinations, and openings/clearances a character can actually traverse. A flat field of arbitrarily placed parts, or a layout with no traversal intent, does not satisfy this.
- **Concrete fix:** Shape the layout around how players move: give the space readable routes and framed approaches to its key destinations, size every doorway/hall/clearance so a character passes comfortably, and avoid both a single undesigned corridor and a flat undirected field — make the geometry guide the player.

## Good (above the mean)

### env-composition-sightlines-landmark-flow

KILLER SYSTEM — COMPOSITION / SIGHTLINES / LANDMARK-GUIDED FLOW. The scene is composed so the player is ALWAYS looking at something intentional: a clear focal landmark anchored by deliberate foreground/midground/background layering, with verticality and sightline-blocking used as directorial tools. To pass, the build must commit to (1) an explicit focal point (landmark/building/statue/skylight) the eye lands on, (2) three staged depth planes (foreground frame e.g. planters/crates, midground subject/walls, background backdrop e.g. distant mountains/hills) with scale-contrast anchors and leading lines, AND (3) sightlines deliberately controlled — elevation changes (main/mezzanine/depressed levels via wedge transitions controlling engagement distance) and/or forced perspective and trees/atmosphere blocking sightlines to manufacture depth and direct the view. A build that is a flat, evenly-distributed field of stuff with no focal hierarchy, no depth layering, and infinite empty space or blank walls FAILS (empty space and blank walls are failures, not neutral).

- **Why above generic:** Generic builds are spatially flat fields of evenly-distributed props with no hierarchy and the eye has nowhere to land; they end abruptly at a flat edge. Deliberate 3-plane staging plus a focal landmark, scale-contrast anchors, verticality controlling sightlines, and sightline-blocking forced perspective is true composition — the recurring hallmark of showcase work and the single fastest read of 'designed' vs 'generated'.
- **Pass:** All three present: an explicit focal landmark the eye lands on, three staged depth planes (foreground frame / midground subject / background backdrop) with scale-contrast anchors and leading lines, AND deliberate sightline control (elevation/wedge level changes and/or forced-perspective sightline-blocking via trees/atmosphere).
- **Partial:** Composition intent is present but incomplete — e.g. a focal point with only two depth planes, or layering without deliberate sightline control, or depth/sightline craft without a clear focal hierarchy.
- **Fail:** A flat, evenly-distributed field of stuff with no focal hierarchy, no depth layering, and infinite empty space or blank walls (empty space and blank walls count as failures, not neutral).
- **Evidence required:** A statement/capture showing an explicit focal landmark, three staged depth planes (foreground/midground/background with scale anchors and leading lines), and deliberate sightline control (elevation/wedge level changes and/or forced-perspective sightline-blocking). A flat evenly-distributed scene with no focal point, no depth planes, or blank empty space does not satisfy this.
- **Concrete fix:** Pick a focal landmark and frame it: place foreground props (planters/crates), midground subject/walls, and a background backdrop (distant hills/mountains); add scale-contrast anchors (towers, skylights) and leading lines; use floor-level changes (main/mezzanine/depressed via wedges) and trees/atmosphere to block sightlines so the player is always looking at something intentional with no blank empty space.

### env-lighting-atmosphere-mood-system

KILLER SYSTEM — LIGHTING / ATMOSPHERE / MOOD. Lighting is an authored mood system tuned together (Lighting + Atmosphere + ambient + color), not engine-default noon. To pass, the build must commit to (1) a committed time-of-day / lighting scenario chosen first (e.g. sunset, evening ClockTime ~17) with the skybox matched to it, (2) a warm-light / cool-shadow color separation — warm highlight tone (ColorShift_Top toward yellow/orange) against a non-black colored shadow fill (Ambient/OutdoorAmbient toward blue/teal, e.g. moonlit (30,30,60) not pure black), and (3) an Atmosphere object tuned for depth/mood (Density ~0.35-0.49 outdoor, low Haze to keep sky clarity, Offset to keep near objects crisp while distant terrain fades, Decay/Color matched to the sky horizon) used as the modern depth-falloff cue. A scene on default white midday light with black shadows and no Atmosphere FAILS.

- **Why above generic:** Engine-default noon lighting with no Atmosphere and pure-black shadows is the #1 signature of an untouched generic build and reads flat/gamey. A committed scenario with warm/cool separation (a real painting fundamental ported to engine settings), colored ambient fill, and a tuned Atmosphere depth cue is what makes a screenshot read as 'a real game' — and it recurs independently across multiple expert sources, with exact reproducible property values.
- **Pass:** All three present: a committed time-of-day/scenario with a matched skybox, warm-highlight vs cool non-black colored-shadow separation (ColorShift_Top warm; Ambient/OutdoorAmbient colored, not black), and a tuned Atmosphere (Density/Haze/Offset/Decay/Color) used for depth/mood.
- **Partial:** Lighting has been deliberately touched but one pillar is missing — e.g. a committed scenario with warm/cool separation but no Atmosphere object, or an Atmosphere present but shadows still left near-black, or a scenario chosen without skybox matching.
- **Fail:** Default white midday light with black shadows and no Atmosphere.
- **Evidence required:** A statement showing a committed time-of-day/scenario with a matched skybox, warm highlight vs cool non-black colored shadow separation (ColorShift_Top warm; Ambient/OutdoorAmbient colored, not black), and a tuned Atmosphere (Density/Haze/Offset/Decay/Color) used for depth. Default midday white light, black shadows, or no Atmosphere does not satisfy this.
- **Concrete fix:** Pick a lighting scenario (e.g. sunset) and match the skybox first; warm the ColorShift_Top toward orange and cool the Ambient/OutdoorAmbient toward blue (never pure black); add an Atmosphere (Density ~0.35-0.49, low Haze, Offset so near objects stay crisp, Decay/Color matched to the horizon) and couple the skybox's lower hemisphere to the ground hue (darker than the upper).

### env-modular-kit-grid-pivot-discipline

A build of unique parts placed at arbitrary coordinates with no shared/reused kit — every part hand-authored — FAILS.

The mean's bare hand-placed parts categorically have no reused kit.
- **Pass:** A kit-based build: a modular kit whose pieces are reused across the build with rotation/scale variation — ideally with full discipline (consistent pivots, module-divisible dimensions on a 5-stud grid, min ~5-stud sizing, mix-and-match child-mesh naming) so pieces tile cleanly.

### env-varied-nonuniform-set-dressing-density

KILLER SYSTEM — MODULAR KIT & LAYERED SET-DRESSING DENSITY (density half). Repeated set dressing is varied and contextually clustered so nothing reads grid-uniform or copy-pasted, with density tuned for curated-not-cluttered. To pass, the build must commit to (1) varying placement, rotation (90deg+), and scale of EVERY repeated element (trees, flowers, barrels, rocks) — ideally via an RNG scatter tool — with per-instance color/tint shifts, exploiting an asset's faces (e.g. rotate a rock for 4 'variations'), never three identical assets in a row, (2) contextual clustering (axes+swords at a market, rock clusters) rather than even spacing, AND (3) detail restraint — concentrating dressing at corners/intersections/T-junctions and leaving deliberate negative space around focal points, avoiding both empty AND over-cluttered noise. A build with uniform spacing/identical rotation/single scale (the procedural-placement tell), OR sprayed-everywhere clutter with no negative space, FAILS.

- **Why above generic:** Uniform spacing, identical rotation, and single scale is THE signature of procedural/AI placement; hand-placed identical props are the generic tell. Randomized transform + per-instance tint + contextual clustering makes a scene feel hand-placed and lived-in, while knowing where to STOP and leave negative space around a focal point (concentrating detail at corners) is taste that distinguishes intentional composition from spray-everything — and defeats both the empty and the cluttered failure modes.
- **Pass:** Varied placement/rotation/scale plus per-instance tint on repeated assets (RNG scatter, no three-in-a-row), contextual clustering instead of even spacing, AND restrained density with negative space and detail concentrated at corners/intersections/T-junctions.
- **Partial:** Variation and clustering are present but density taste is off — e.g. varied transforms but still sprayed everywhere with no negative space, or restrained but with some identical-in-a-row repetition, or clustering without per-instance tint.
- **Fail:** Uniform spacing / identical rotation / single scale (the procedural-placement tell), or sprayed-everywhere clutter with no negative space.
- **Evidence required:** A statement showing varied placement/rotation/scale + per-instance tint on repeated assets (RNG scatter, no three-in-a-row), contextual clustering instead of even spacing, AND restrained density with negative space and detail concentrated at corners/intersections. Grid-uniform identical placement, or sprayed clutter with no negative space, does not satisfy this.
- **Concrete fix:** Scatter repeated assets with an RNG tool varying rotation (90deg+), scale, and per-instance tint (rotate a rock's faces for free variation, never three identical in a row), cluster props contextually (market wares, rock clusters), and concentrate detail at corners/T-junctions while leaving negative space around focal points — enough to feel curated, not cluttered.

### env-human-scale-proportion-standard
